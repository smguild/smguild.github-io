01 — Context

Redesigning educational materials for flight attendants

Crew Duties: Phase 1 is one of the first training presentations that new hire flight attendants receive — delivered on Day 2 of their initial onboarding program. At four hours and 130 slides, the instructor-led course establishes a baseline understanding of roles, responsibilities, safety procedures, and in-flight service standards for incoming cabin crew.

Because flight attendants are held to strict FAA compliance standards, the accuracy and clarity of this material directly affects both safety outcomes and regulatory standing.

"Redesign the Crew Duties: Phase 1 training to promote learning through an interactive, engaging presentation that supports concept retention and FAA regulatory compliance."
Project Goal
02 — Process

Observation & discovery

The redesign began with direct observation of a live Crew Duties training session. I attended a full four-hour delivery of the training to document how students engaged with the material, where instructors had to compensate for slide limitations, and where pacing broke down.

Findings were cross-referenced against the Inflight Procedures Manual (IPM) and current FAA regulatory requirements to ensure any changes preserved and improved compliance alignment.

03 — Findings

Areas for improvement

After observing the training delivery, three core issues emerged consistently across the session.

F1 Repetitive slide template. Every slide used the identical layout, making the content visually monotonous and reducing student anticipation or engagement with each new screen. Varied layouts, progressive reveals, and animated transitions were needed to restore pacing variety.
F2 Photo-only slides with no on-screen text. Many slides displayed a single photograph while the instructor read from notes, leaving students with no anchoring text to reference or retain. Without visible text, students depended entirely on working memory during instruction — reducing recall of critical safety and service procedures.
F3 Difficulty referencing the IPM. Instructors regularly asked students to locate specific chapters and sections in their Inflight Procedures Manual. Students frequently struggled to find the correct reference quickly, disrupting class flow. Adding direct IPM citations to applicable slides would reduce friction and keep the session on schedule.
04 — Redesign (Selected Works)

Targeted design changes

The redesign addressed each identified gap through four representative slide sections — each demonstrating a distinct intervention. All changes were made within the existing Envoy brand framework and subject to FAA compliance review.

D1 Customer Preboarding — Smooth Transitions & Minimal Text. Slide transitions were redesigned to be smooth and logical, guiding students through content without abrupt shifts. Minimal supporting text was added to anchor key points while allowing the instructor to hold student focus.

D2 Originating Flights — Sequential Concept Reveals. Content covering originating flights, late inbound coordination, and gate agent communication was restructured so each concept appeared on its own slide and was revealed one-by-one — eliminating information overload and directing student attention.

05 — Outcome

Impact & reflection

This project required rapidly building domain expertise in an unfamiliar industry. I learned flight attendant roles, responsibilities, acronyms, and FAA safety standards within weeks. That learning curve reinforced a core UX principle: proximity to subject-matter experts is essential, and immersion accelerates understanding.

Redesigning 130 slides under a compressed timeline also strengthened time management and prioritization skills, making scope and sequencing decisions quickly while maintaining quality standards. The internship has sparked an ongoing interest in pursuing UX work within the aviation industry.
