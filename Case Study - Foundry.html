01 — Background

How do users find and select the right model for their use case?

Microsoft Foundry is an AI platform that gives users access to a catalog of thousands of machine learning models. Following a major redesign of the homepage, it became unclear how well the redesigned interface supported developers in finding and selecting the right model.

02 — Research Questions

What I set out to learn

I wanted to understand user's choices behind selecting an AI model and how that informs their actions on Foundry. These questions were chosen to understand the full discovery journey, from first entry point to final model selection.

RQ1 How do users currently navigate to and within the Discover section?
RQ2 What mental models do users bring to model search, comparison, and evaluation?
RQ3 What comparison tools do users notice or use during their decision-making process?
RQ4 What information do users need before they feel confident selecting a model for their use case?
03 — Method

Study design

I conducted 8 moderated usability sessions over two weeks via video call, using a think-aloud protocol. Each 60-minute session included 4 task scenarios, a post-task difficulty rating (SEQ), and a 5-minute debrief. Sessions were recorded and auto-transcribed using Dovetail.

Foundry Target Audience

Participants were recruited across three user segments: software engineers, ML engineers, and students with experience developing AI agents.

Mapped all user interaction paths to understand the experience, revealing three separate model comparison entry points.

04 — Key Findings

Observed behaviors

Across all 8 sessions, three critical failure patterns emerged consistently — regardless of user segment or task.

F1 Key discovery tools were difficult to find or went entirely unnoticed. Half of all participants never used the AI-powered search bar, and 4 out of 8 expressed wanting an AI assistant to guide their exploration—unaware one already existed. The Compare Models tool, though well-received once found, was missed entirely by 1 participant and took over 3 minutes for 2 others to locate.
F2 Users lacked clarity and context around important model information. 6 out of 8 users wanted more context around benchmark and endpoint metrics. Users spent an average of 7 minutes on Compare Models versus under 3 minutes on Model Details, suggesting that visual comparison charts were driving decisions more than detailed documentation.
F3 Model recency and deployment pathways were unclear to users. All 8 participants gravitated toward recent frontier models but struggled to assess recency from model names alone.
“So many models to choose from. If only there was a way for me to share my use case with an AI model built in... and have it recommend me a model.”

05 — Recommendations

From insight to action

I delivered recommendations based on severity: how often the issue occured and its impact on discovery flow.

R1 Surface a "Find models" entry point above the fold on the Foundry homepage — distinct from global search, with clear affordance.
R2 Add a Compare Models CTA directly on the Model Details page to allow users to compare potential models instantly without disrupting their search.
R3 Surface the AI chatbot as a distinct, prominent button to increase usage and make the feature immediately recognizable.
R4 Contextualize benchmark metrics with tooltips: to reduce jargon-based confusion and keep users from having to leave Foundry to find answers.
Selected quantitative analysis notes, including time on task, task completion and confidence rating.

06 — Impact

What changed

Following the delivery of our findings, the Microsoft Core AI team made a notable update to Foundry's homepage — adding a dedicated "Find models" callout that directly surfaces the path to model exploration for new users.  This change directly addresses one of our study's core insights: that users struggled to orient themselves on the platform and find their way to the Discover page. 

One limitation of this study was our lack of AI credits to support the deployment of models, which caused participants to stop before deploying a model. If I were to do the study again, I would add AI credits to explore user behavior in the deployment phase and test across both light and dark modes on Foundry to capture differences in user actions.
